# Deep Reviews — 2026-10-02 pass

Five reviews, each written as an attempt to refute the pass rather than to confirm it. A review
that found nothing is recorded as finding nothing, with what would have had to be true for it to
fire. Verdicts are PASS, PASS WITH DISCLOSURE, or PARTIAL.

The pass's central claim: **a $/M token price is not a measurement until the traffic mix it assumes
is stated, and stating it changes the answer by 5x to 29x depending on the model.** Everything below attacks that claim or
the evidence under it.

---

## Review 1 — Re-derivation from the archived bytes, not from the report

Recomputed every figure in `data/plan-economics.csv` from the fetched pages, ignoring the report
text entirely.

**Method.** Z.ai's published credit formula and multiplier table were applied to its published
weekly credit pools, and the resulting token counts were compared with the vendor's own "Estimated
Token Allowance" table. The OpenCode Go grid was re-parsed from `sources/opencode-go.html` with the
shipped parser and diffed against the committed CSV.

**Findings.**

1. All six Z.ai rows match the vendor's table exactly. GLM-5.3-Flash Lite: 292 M tok/week
   (95% cache, off-peak) x 4.33 = 1,264 M, and $18 / 1,264 M = $0.0142/M. The peak floor
   146 M x 4.33 = 632 M at $0.0285/M. The vendor's own 96% and 98% rows move to 1,299 M and
   1,373 M, which is the cache sensitivity the report claims and is visible in their table rather
   than inferred.
2. `tools/parse-opencode-go.py` re-run against the archived `opencode-go.html` reproduces
   `data/opencode-go-grid.csv` **byte for byte** (78 rows, 39 per plan). The dataset is
   regenerable from the evidence in the repo, which is the property that makes it worth keeping.
3. The three-mix table in the report is the shipped parser's `blended_per_m` column recomputed at
   50/40/10 and 0/75/25. Spot-checked by hand: Muse Spark 1.3 Contributor is
   0.97x0.002 + 0.025x0.10 + 0.005x0.20 = $0.0054/M blended; $10 / ($60 / 0.0054) = $0.0009/M.
   The no-cache column is 0.75x0.10 + 0.25x0.20 = $0.125/M, giving $10 / ($60/0.125) = $0.0208/M,
   which is the figure the fork published and then declared wrong. Both confirmed.

**Verdict: PASS.** No figure in the pass is unreproducible from the archived sources.

## Review 2 — Falsification of the headline claim

The obvious attack: the mix is a free parameter, so the widest spread is an artifact of choosing
extremes, and the real answer is somewhere in the middle and much less dramatic.

**What would make the claim false.** If the vendor's grid implied a single mix regardless of
assumption, the spread would collapse and the claim would be a nuisance rather than a finding.

**Tested.** It does not. The OpenCode Go grid publishes four separate prices per model (input,
output, cached read, cached write). Those prices are not proportional, so the blended price moves
with the weights rather than being invariant. The cheapest model on the plan, Muse Spark 1.3
Contributor, prices input at $0.10 and output at $0.20 with a cached read at $0.002, a 100x spread
between the cheapest and dearest token class, which is why its $/M moves 23x across mixes. MiMo-V2.6-Pro has a wider gap still and moves 29x.
GLM-5.3-Flash moves only 6.7x over the same range because its spread is narrower. Across the 37
priced models the ratio runs 5.3x to 29.0x with a median of 13.6x, so the effect is the rule for
this plan rather than a property of one row. The claim is not a
property of picking bad mixes; it is a property of how differently vendors price cache reads.

**Counter-test run, and its result.** I also checked the obvious alternative explanation: that the
sensitivity is really about the *plan* rather than the mix. It is not. On a fixed mix,
GLM-5.3-Flash costs $0.0142/M via Z.ai credits and $0.0059/M via an OpenCode Go dollar ceiling,
a 2.4x difference between two vendors for the same model, which is a different axis entirely and is
reported separately.

**Finding.** The claim survives. A reader who wants a single number still has to pick a mix, and
the repo now makes that choice visible instead of burying it.

**Verdict: PASS.**

## Review 3 — Audit of the fork's inherited claims

Every claim taken from the fork's 2026-10-02 pass, checked against its own cited evidence.

**Findings.**

1. **Reproduced.** The anonymous-access probe was re-run today using the fork's own unmodified
   script: 25 endpoints, three rounds, no credential. The verdict matched on all 25. Three
   endpoints changed status code (Groq, Token Harbor and Together 401 instead of 403) behind a
   CDN, with no change in class. The "1 usable endpoint in 25" headline stands.
2. **Refuted.** The "transparency defect" claim. See the report for the control: an identical
   27k-character prompt returns `finish_reason=length` with empty content at `max_tokens` 16/32/64
   and `finish_reason=stop` with the needle retrieved at 256/1024. Billing tracks chars/token
   linearly from 1,655 tokens at 7.4k characters to 74,245 at 370k. The 89,063-vs-400,000-char
   discrepancy the fork describes is not present in my measurements; on that same prompt size I
   measure 4.11 chars/token. The underlying observation worth keeping is that a small
   `max_tokens` on a reasoning model returns an empty answer while still billing the prompt, and
   the report states it that way.
3. **Refuted as stated.** The "16x correction" on OpenCode Go. $0.0013/M and $0.0208/M are the same
   method under two mixes, not a right answer and a wrong one. The report says so and shows the
   arithmetic.
4. **Data errors in the fork's cost table.** Claude Pro is published at 2,300M-3,054M tokens and
   $0.0087/M; the cited `adopted.csv` row for `claude-opus-5.5` at $20 reads 3,054,000,000 and
   $0.0065488. Claude Max 20x is published at 3,054M-3,810M and $0.0655/M; the cited rows for that
   plan read 6,690,000,000 / $0.0299, 31,538,000,000 / $0.0063 and 5,052,000,000 / $0.0396. The
   published Max row is about 2x the value it carries, and it is labelled `MEDIUM-CORRECTED` while
   disagreeing with its own source. I did not adopt those rows. I did not "correct" them either:
   the correct value is ambiguous, because the source offers three Max figures for three different
   measurement dates, and picking one silently would repeat the error.
5. **Confirmed.** The twelve GLM rows in the fork's table match my independent derivation to the
   digit, so the fork's credit arithmetic is sound even where its transcription is not.
6. **Not verified.** The fork's 10-03 branch claims 55 sources fetched on 2026-10-03 while this
   machine's clock reads 2026-10-02, and its `fetch-log.json` carries no per-entry timestamps, so
   nothing in it corroborates the date. Its Cursor Pro+ claim I did verify independently from
   Cursor's own schema.org block, and it is correct. The rest of that branch is not adopted.

**Verdict: PASS WITH DISCLOSURE.** The fork's core measurement is sound and reproduced. Its
headline interpretation was wrong, its cost table contains transcription errors, and one of its
unmerged branches is dated in a way its own log cannot support.

## Review 4 — Security, honesty and privacy

Read as an adversarial reader and as the vendor's own counsel.

**Findings.**

1. **No credentials anywhere in the repo, and the probe cannot acquire one.** The Zen probe
   constructs every request with an explicit header set that contains no `Authorization` entry, so
   it cannot pick one up from the environment even by accident. Verified by reading the script and
   by the fetch log, which records no auth-bearing URLs.
2. **The probe does not degrade the service.** It sent 12 models x 3 rounds plus a nine-point size
   sweep plus a five-round control: roughly 60 requests to a free endpoint in a few minutes. That
   is probe volume, not load, and the report does not claim otherwise.
3. **The `max_tokens` control is disclosed as mine, not as a vendor behaviour.** A reader could
   easily misread "the model returned nothing" as a capability finding. The report states the
   empty completion is a consequence of the caller's budget and shows the control.
4. **One reading the fork did not surface and this pass does not either:** the Z.ai Team Plan page
   states that code, prompts and conversations are excluded from model training by default, while
   the individual plan's terms are not read here. The report notes the training exclusion as a
   purchasing fact and does not generalise it to other plans.
5. **No resale, no shared accounts, no ToS-violating channel is recommended anywhere.** The relay
   and reseller universe stayed excluded, as the 2026-09-20 pass set that policy.

**Verdict: PASS.**

## Review 5 — Attack the validator, because a gate nobody has seen refuse is not a gate

`tools/validate.py` is the thing that makes this pass different from the fork's, so it gets the
same treatment as the data.

**Findings.**

1. **It found 7 real defects in this repo's own history.** Running it with `--all` against the
   pre-fix tree reports malformed rows in `2026-09-13/data/providers-database.csv` (2) and
   `2026-09-20/data/agents-universe.csv` (1) and `2026-09-20/data/delta-vs-2026-09-13.csv` (4).
   Every one is an unquoted comma inside a value, which shifts every column after it. The previous
   validator reported both passes as clean.
2. **The fork's validator reports 38 failures on the same tree; 31 are false positives** on prose
   ledgers whose first line is a `#` comment, and 7 are the real ones above. Its prose-ledger skip
   is a fix I did not inherit, because my own `read_rows` skips the preamble by design.
3. **It refuses a planted cost error.** A row stating $10 / 480M tokens / $0.0013-M is reported as
   `16.03x off`, which is the same factor as the disputed OpenCode row, so the check is sensitive
   at the magnitude that matters.
4. **It refuses a planted column shift** and **a planted unsourced number**, and accepts a correct
   row with exit 0. A guard that refuses everything is indistinguishable from a working one until
   the accepting case is run, so all four cases are in this document.
5. **It caught three defects in this pass's own new data**, all of the same class it was written
   for: unquoted commas in `plan-economics.csv`. They were found before commit rather than after.
6. **It caught a wrong row count in this pass's report** (said 76, file has 78) after a
   `report-matches-data` check was added, which is why that check exists.
7. **Two bugs in the validator itself were found and fixed during this review**: a `relative_to`
   crash when a pass directory is passed as a relative path, and a cost check that matched columns
   by substring and so compared a plan price against a per-token rate, producing 95 confident
   nonsense failures. The second is worth recording as a class: a check that is confidently wrong
   is worse than no check, and the way it surfaced was the same run that validated good data.

**Verdict: PASS.**

## What this pass did not establish

- No plan was subscribed to and no authenticated request was made, so nothing here is a metered
  result. Every allowance is a published ceiling.
- The traffic mix is a quoted convention from `real-api-pricing`, not a measurement of this
  pass's own workload. The report states the mix beside every figure so the assumption is visible.
- The OpenCode Go monthly-vs-weekly inconsistency is reported as an inconsistency. Which reading is
  correct is not decidable from the page, and this pass does not guess.
- The ~30-domain relay/reseller universe was not re-verified and is out of scope.
- The fork's 10-03 branch is not adopted; its date is not corroborated by its own log.
