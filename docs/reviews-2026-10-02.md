# Deep Reviews — 2026-10-02 pass

Seven reviews, each testing a claim in the pass against evidence rather than confirming it. Verdicts
are PASS, PASS WITH DISCLOSURE, or PARTIAL. A review that found nothing states what would have had
to be true for it to fire.

---

## Review 1 — Re-derivation from the archived bytes

Every figure in [data/plan-economics.csv](../2026-10-02/data/plan-economics.csv) recomputed from
the fetched pages, ignoring the report text.

**Findings.**

1. All six Z.ai rows reproduce from the vendor's own credit table. GLM-5.3-Flash Lite: 292 M
   tok/week (95% cache, off-peak ceiling) x 4.33 = 1,264 M, and $18 / 1,264 M = $0.0142/M. Peak
   floor 146 M x 4.33 = 632 M at $0.0285/M. The vendor's own 96% and 98% rows move to 1,299 M and
   1,373 M, which is the cache sensitivity section 1 of the report claims, visible in their table
   rather than inferred.
2. `tools/parse-opencode-go.py` re-run against the archived `opencode-go.html` reproduces
   [data/opencode-go-grid.csv](../2026-10-02/data/opencode-go-grid.csv) **byte for byte** (78
   rows, 39 per plan). The dataset regenerates from evidence in the repo, which is the property
   that makes it citable.
3. The three-mix table is the parser's blended column recomputed at 50/40/10 and 0/75/25.
   Spot-checked by hand: Muse Spark 1.3 Contributor is 0.97x0.002 + 0.025x0.10 + 0.005x0.20 =
   $0.0054/M, so $10 / ($60 / 0.0054) = $0.0009/M. The no-cache column is 0.75x0.10 + 0.25x0.20 =
   $0.125/M, giving $10 / ($60 / 0.125) = $0.0208/M.

**Verdict: PASS.** No figure is unreproducible from the archived sources.

## Review 2 — Falsification of the central claim

**Claim under test.** A $/M figure is not a measurement until its traffic mix is stated, and stating
the mix changes the answer by 5.3x to 29.0x.

**Strongest counter-hypothesis:** the mix is a free parameter, so the widest spread is an artifact of
choosing extremes and the real answer sits nearer the middle.

**What would make the claim false.** If the vendor's grid implied one mix regardless of assumption,
the spread would collapse.

**Tested.** It does not. The grid publishes four separate token prices per model, and they are not
proportional, so the blended price moves with the weights rather than being invariant. The
cheapest model on the plan prices input at $0.10 and output at $0.20 against a cached read of
$0.002, a 100x spread between the dearest and cheapest token class, and its $/M moves 23x across
mixes. MiMo-V2.6-Pro has a wider gap and moves 29x. GLM-5.3-Flash moves 6.7x because its spread is
narrower. Across the 37 priced models per plan the ratio runs 5.3x to 29.0x, median 13.6x
([data/method-sensitivity.csv](../2026-10-02/data/method-sensitivity.csv)). The claim is a property
of how vendors price cache reads, not of the mix chosen.

**Counter-test, and its result.** The alternative explanation, that the sensitivity is really about
the plan rather than the mix, was tested and rejected: on a fixed mix GLM-5.3-Flash costs
$0.0142/M via Z.ai credits and $0.0059/M via an OpenCode Go dollar ceiling, a 2.4x difference
between two vendors for the same model. That is a different axis and is reported separately.

**Second counter-hypothesis:** a cache-heavy mix is the right *default* only if it is observed rather
than convenient. A vendor varying its own published ceiling across three cache hit rates, and metered
runs recording 91.97% to 96.9% cache-read share, both establish it as the observed case.

**Verdict: PASS.**

## Review 3 — Audit of the third-party measurements carried in section 6

[data/subscription-measurements.csv](../2026-10-02/data/subscription-measurements.csv) carries
metered multipliers attributed to an external measurement project. Ranking them next to published
ceilings in one table would compare two evidence classes as if they were one quantity, so they are
carried separately and not ranked. That decision was tested.

**Findings.**

1. **The method is sound and was checked rather than trusted.** Recomputing the SuperGrok run's
   per-call cost from its raw call log at its own stated list price gives $67.79 against a
   published $68.86, a 1.6% gap attributable to calls crossing a token-threshold price step. The
   method survives independent recomputation.
2. **The multiplier is bracketing-sensitive and the report must say so.** A whole-run recomputation
   of the same SuperGrok log gives 246x against the source's 190x. The difference is the source's
   documented choice to bracket only spend between meter ticks, in its shared method step 3. The
   246x figure is recorded in the database next to the 190x so the sensitivity is visible rather
   than hidden in a footnote.
3. **The class distinction is the finding, not a hedge.** A multiplier is one plan, one workload,
   one day, one account's meter. This pass subscribed to nothing, so its own allowances are
   published ceilings. Presenting the two side by side as a ranking is the error that produces an
   unfalsifiable market table, and section 4 of the report declines to commit it.
4. **One figure is a derived row, not a measurement**, and is labelled `(whole-run
   recomputation)` in the plan column so it cannot be mistaken for a published result.

**Verdict: PASS WITH DISCLOSURE.** The measurements are attributed, dated, method-stated and
bracketed, and the one figure this pass produced itself is labelled as its own.

## Review 4 — Audit of the free-tier access measurement

Section 2's claim is that price and access are different properties, evidenced by 1 of 25 endpoints
and 1 of 12 free-listed models answering anonymously.

**Findings.**

1. **The probe cannot cheat.** Every request is built with an explicit header set containing no
   `Authorization` entry, so it cannot acquire a credential from the environment, a file or a
   prompt. Read in the source and confirmed in the fetch log, which records no auth-bearing URLs.
2. **The control that shows the test can detect retrieval.** The same model returns a correct diff
   for a real bug fix (`return a-b` to `return a+b`) and retrieves a needle at 7.4k, 27.8k, 55.6k,
   111k, 231k, 278k and 370k characters. A test that cannot fire is indistinguishable from a test
   that passes, so the positive cases are recorded alongside the negative ones.
3. **The one caveat is a caller parameter and is labelled as such.** The endpoint spends its
   completion budget on `reasoning_content`; with an identical prompt, `finish_reason` is `length`
   and content empty at `max_tokens` 16/32/64, and `stop` with correct content at 256/1024. The
   report states this as a property of the request, because a reader could otherwise read "returned
   an empty answer" as a capability finding. Billing tracks chars/token linearly from 1,655 tokens
   at 7.4k characters to 74,245 at 370k, so reported usage is usable for budgeting.
4. **Service impact.** 12 models x 3 rounds, a nine-point size sweep and a five-round control is
   roughly 60 requests to a free endpoint in a few minutes. Probe volume, not load, and the report
   claims nothing more than that.
5. **The claim is scoped to what was measured.** One vantage point, one afternoon, no authenticated
   path. An `AUTH_REQUIRED` row means gated, not expensive, and the report says so.

**Verdict: PASS.**

## Review 5 — Attack the validator

`tools/validate.py` gates every pass, so it received the same treatment as the data.

**Findings.**

1. **It found 7 real defects in this repo's own data**, all unquoted commas inside a value
   shifting every later CSV column: two rows in `2026-09-13/data/providers-database.csv`, one in
   `2026-09-20/data/agents-universe.csv`, four in
   `2026-09-20/data/delta-vs-2026-09-13.csv`. The validator in use at the time reported both passes
   as clean.
2. **The CI workflow validated only the newest pass directory**, so even a corrected historical
   file would not have been re-checked on a later push. It now runs every pass, plus a check that
   the OpenCode Go grid regenerates byte for byte from the archived page.
3. **It refuses a planted cost error.** A row stating $10 / 480M tokens / $0.0013-M is reported as
   `16.03x off`, which is the magnitude of the error class this pass is about, so the check is
   sensitive where it needs to be.
4. **It refuses a planted column shift and a planted unsourced number, and accepts a correct row
   with exit 0.** A guard that refuses everything is indistinguishable from a working one until the
   accepting case is run, so all four cases are recorded here.
5. **It caught three defects in this pass's own new data**, all of the class it was written for:
   unquoted commas in `plan-economics.csv`, found before commit rather than after.
6. **It caught a stale row count in this pass's report** (said 19, file had 22) after a
   `report-matches-data` check was added. That check is why the number cannot drift again.
7. **Two bugs in the validator itself were found and fixed during this review**: a `relative_to`
   crash when a pass directory is passed as a relative path, and a cost check that matched columns
   by substring and so compared a plan price against a per-token rate, producing 95 confident
   nonsense failures. The second is the more instructive: a check that is confidently wrong is
   worse than no check, and the only thing that surfaced it was running the check against known-good
   data. Column matching is exact-name only for that reason.

**Verdict: PASS.**

## Review 6 — The ranked field, and a defect that made it empty

The pass's earlier structure answered a methodological question and stopped there. It carried no
model landscape, no ranking and no recommendation, so a reader could not act on any of it. The
earlier passes in this repo answer "what should I buy" and this one did not. That is a gap in the
pass, not a stylistic preference.

**What was added.** The Artificial Analysis leaderboard archived in `sources/aa-models.html` is now
mined into `data/models-database.csv`: 24 models with Intelligence Index, context window, modalities
and API list price. Combined with `plan-economics.csv` it yields 12 plan/model pairs where both a
first-party ceiling and a leaderboard score exist, ranked by quality per dollar, plus a 52.5M-token
workload test and a purchase recommendation.

**The defect this review was written to record.** The first attempt at that ranking returned an
**empty table**, silently. `plan-economics.csv` carries display names ("GLM-5.3-Flash") and the
model database carries slugs ("glm-5-3-flash"), so the join matched nothing and produced no rows
with no error raised. A ranking that is empty looks exactly like a ranking that found nothing, and
nothing in the pass would have said otherwise. Two fixes: a `model_slug` column with unmappable
names left as UNKNOWN rather than guessed, and a `model-slug-joins` validator check that fails any
plan row naming a model absent from the landscape. The check was verified by planting a typo'd slug
and reading the failure, and by confirming it passes on the corrected data.

**Three claims the added section got wrong on first write, found by re-deriving every figure from
the data files rather than from the prose:**

1. "Six of the top seven models were released in September." All seven were. Corrected.
2. "23 of 24 models have a 1M or near-1M context window." 17 of 24 have a full 1M; 19 of 24 have at
   least 512k. The original figure was reached by reading "near-1M" as inclusive of 500k, which it is
   not. Corrected, and the five exceptions are now named.
3. "A gap of 7.3 II points to the cheapest frontier model," and later "7.7 points to the best
   non-Anthropic entry." The gaps are 9.53 and 4.47 respectively. Corrected.

**A fourth, dismissed after checking.** The workload table showed K2 Horizon at a $0.00 list cost,
which would have made it the cheapest way to run 52.5M tokens. It is open weights with no published
API tariff, not a free API, so it is excluded from the pass/fail reading and labelled in place. A
zero in a price column is a missing price, and the table now says so.

**The extraction itself was checked for silent truncation.** The flight payload contains 48 objects
carrying a UUID `id` and a slug, of which 24 are models (they carry an Intelligence Index) and 24
are providers (they do not). An early version recovered only the 24 models and was suspected of
dropping half the set; counting providers separately confirmed 24 + 24 and no loss. The parser
extracts the model landscape byte for byte from the archived page, and CI fails if it stops doing so.

**Verdict: PASS.** The ranked field is built from data that regenerates from evidence in the repo,
and the three factual errors found in it are corrected in the report.

## Review 7 — Extending coverage, and a provenance error the extension found

The pass rested its central claim on one vendor's price table and mined 4 of its 20 archived
sources. Both are weaknesses a reader is entitled to see tested rather than assumed.

**What was added.** `tools/extract-plans.py` and `data/plan-ladder.csv`: 30 plans across 7 vendors
read from 7 pages, up from 22 rows on 5 vendors. `tools/extract-token-prices.py` and
`data/openai-token-prices.csv`: 7 models from OpenAI's own platform pricing page, which publishes
the same four-column shape the method needs. `data/mix-sensitivity-all.csv` then computes the
sensitivity across both vendors and both evidence classes.

**Whether the method survives a second vendor.** Yes, and the numbers are the evidence rather than
the assertion. Across 44 model/price-class rows: subscription-ceiling rows span 5.3x to 29.0x with
a median of 13.6x, API-list rows span 1.7x to 20.3x with a median of 13.6x, and the combined median
is 13.6x. Two vendors, two evidence classes, a 20x difference in absolute price, identical median.
As a cross-check on the landscape itself, OpenAI's published prices agree with the leaderboard's
list prices to the cent for both models the two sources share (gpt-6-astra $10/$50, gpt-6-luna
$0.10/$0.50).

**The 1.7x floor was investigated rather than reported as a weaker result.** Both rows at the
bottom are OpenAI's two transcribe models, which publish no cached tier at all. Their spread is
computed with the input price standing in for a cache price that does not exist, so it is not a
measured cache sensitivity. The column `cached_tier_published` now records `no` for them, and the
report says a vendor publishing no cache price cannot be evaluated on cache sensitivity.

**A provenance error this extension found, which is the reason the extension was worth doing.**
Verifying every ladder price against the bytes of the page it cites found that **Z.ai's overview
page publishes only one price**, "starting at just 18 USD per month". The Pro ($72) and Max ($160)
figures attributed to that page appear nowhere in it, on the FAQ page, or on the Team Plan page
archived here. They came from the 2026-09-20 pass. Four rows in `plan-economics.csv` therefore
claimed `FIRST-PARTY-COMPUTED` for a price this pass could not verify from its own sources, and the
$0.0142/M and $0.0090/M figures derived from them inherited that provenance. Those rows are now
`CARRIED-FORWARD`, the ladder rows are `NOT-ON-PAGE`, and the report states the distinction: the
arithmetic is correct and rests on a price this pass could not re-verify, which is a different
statement from "verified".

**That check is now permanent.** `validate.py` gained `ladder-price-on-page`, which reads each cited
page and fails any row whose price is absent from it unless the row is marked
`NOT-ON-PAGE` / `CARRIED-FORWARD` / `NOT-PUBLISHED`. It was verified by re-planting the original
error and reading the failure.

**Two bugs in the new tooling and in the new check, both found by running them rather than by
reading them.**

1. `extract-plans.py` in its first form matched any text near a price and produced "Memory across
   conversations", "per month" and "Compare features" as plans, 16 rows from one page. A loose
   extractor is worse than none, because a table of junk reads as coverage. It now requires a short
   Title-Case name with no sentence punctuation, and rejects feature labels. A page that yields
   nothing is reported as yielding nothing.
2. The `ladder-price-on-page` check itself was wrong twice. It first read only `$N` and so reported
   correct rows as missing, because vendors also write `18 USD` in prose and `"price":18` in a
   JSON-LD block. Having fixed that, it still reported 10 correct rows as missing because
   `rstrip("0")` on `"10"` yields `"1"`. A check that is confidently wrong is worse than no check,
   and both bugs surfaced only from running it against known-good data.

**Extraction was checked for silent loss.** The OpenAI grid is under a `colspan` group header
('Short context' / 'Long context'), so reading the first `<tr>` as the header yields group labels
instead of column names and the table is skipped entirely. The first run recovered 2 transcribe
models and none of the gpt-6 rows. The extractor now collects the leading run of `<th>` rows and
recovers 7. The same check was run on the leaderboard: 48 UUID-slug objects in the payload are 24
models and 24 providers, and the 24 recovered are all of them.

**Verdict: PASS WITH DISCLOSURE.** The method is now demonstrated across two vendors and both
evidence classes, coverage rose from 4 mined sources to 7, and the extension surfaced a
first-party attribution error in the pass's own table that is now corrected and guarded.

## What this pass did not establish

- No plan was subscribed to and no authenticated request was made. Every allowance is a published
  ceiling, and no figure here is a metered result.
- The traffic mix is a quoted convention from a third-party conventions file, not a measurement of
  this pass's own workload. It is stated beside every figure that depends on it.
- OpenCode Go's monthly-versus-weekly request columns are irreconcilable at 2.00x against 4.33
  weeks, and the page does not say which is authoritative. Not guessed.
- Fifteen of the twenty fetched sources carry no figure here. They are archived as the
  re-verification base, and the report states the count rather than implying full coverage.
- The relay and reseller universe is out of scope and not re-verified.
- The leaderboard's Terminal-Bench 4.0 column is empty for all 24 models in today's payload, so the
  ranking uses the Intelligence Index alone. The Intelligence Index is a composite and is not an
  agentic-tooling benchmark, so "II 48.09" should be read as quality per published list price rather
  than as a claim about agent task performance.
- Free-tier entries in the short answer (Antigravity, Kiro Free, Command Code Go at $1) are carried
  forward from the 2026-09-20 pass and were not re-verified today. The only free surface measured
  in this pass is the anonymous probe in section 2.
- Seven of the twenty archived sources still carry no figure, and the report names which seven
  rather than leaving a reader to count. MiniMax is the largest omission: its token plan is a paid
  subscription with a published price and no published token allowance, so it needs a fetch of a
  page this pass did not archive.
- The two Z.ai prices behind the $0.0142/M and $0.0090/M rows are carried forward, not verified.
