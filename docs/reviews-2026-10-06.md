## Review 1 — The 190x multiplier is retracted by its own source

**Defect.** [../2026-10-02/README.md](../2026-10-02/README.md) §7 and
[../docs/reviews-2026-10-02.md](reviews-2026-10-02.md) Review 3 published "SuperGrok 190x ±21", and
`2026-10-02/data/subscription-measurements.csv` carried the row `SuperGrok,xAI,30,190,21`.

**How it was found.** The upstream project `phuryn/experiments` was re-read, and its commit log
carries a retraction the 2026-10-02 pass had no reason to see yet:

```
$ gh api repos/phuryn/experiments/commits --jq '.[] | {sha: .sha[0:9], date: .commit.author.date, msg: (.commit.message|split("\n")[0])}'
{"date":"2026-10-03T19:23:49Z","msg":"subscription-multipliers: SuperGrok on a clean account (no X linked) = 18.0x; X-linked account reported as SuperGrok + X Premium+ ($70) = 80x (186x vs SuperGrok price alone)","sha":"441931da3"}
{"date":"2026-10-03T08:55:14Z","msg":"subscription-multipliers: X Premium+ grants SuperGrok-level Grok access when linked (was: 'includes SuperGrok'); source linked","sha":"67c658054"}
{"date":"2026-10-01T16:30:38Z","msg":"subscription-multipliers: SuperGrok text-only run (fresh random text, no cross-prompt cache) - 190x +-21 across image and text workloads; chart with uncertainty bars","sha":"86739c321"}
```

The retracting README text, read today:

> "**Read with [07](../07-supergrok-no-x/).** The same plan on a fresh account with no X account
> linked measured **18.0×**. This account got ~10× that allowance. Priced at the $70 it pays for
> SuperGrok and X Premium+ together, it is **80× ± 4**; against the SuperGrok price alone, 186× ± 10."

**Verdict: the 190x is retracted and the pass published it three times.** The 2026-10-02 pass had
already *noticed* the confound — its Review 3 carried the linked-subscription caveat as item 2 of
findings — and then published the number anyway in the report, in the review, and in the CSV. A
caveat attached to a figure is not a correction to it, and a reader taking the headline column is
entitled to read it as verified.

**Correction made.** The 190x row and its 246x whole-run re-derivation are kept and marked
`SUPERSEDED`; the current figures are new rows in
[../2026-10-06/data/subscription-measurements.csv](../2026-10-06/data/subscription-measurements.csv).
Both `2026-10-02` documents carry a dated superseding note naming the corrected figures, because
deleting the number would remove the evidence that the correction happened.

**Magnitude of the confound, which is the transferable part.** The account that produced 190x got
about 10x a plain SuperGrok week's allowance (18.0x vs 80x at comparable price bases), and the price
basis moved separately (190x was against the $30 plan price; 80x is against the $70 that pays for
both subscriptions). Two independent effects, ~10x and ~2.3x. **Any subscription multiplier read
from one account is an upper bound until its confounds are enumerated**, and this one survived a
pass that had written the confound down.

## Review 2 — OpenCode Go's $60 is a shared pool, not per-model budgets

**Defect.** The 2026-10-02 pass read the vendor grid as independent per-model ceilings and published
its top row as "Muse Spark 1.3 Contributor, II 48.09, $0.0009/M, 53,436 II-points per $/M" — the
whole month spent on one model. `2026-09-20` reached the same reading earlier and published it as
"up to ~$60/mo of list-value usage **across 27 models**", "~6× face".

**The vendor's sentence does not support either reading.** [../2026-10-06/sources/opencode-go.md](../2026-10-06/sources/opencode-go.md),
fetched 2026-10-06, HTTP 200, 39,692 B, `sha256:e62561c5c6fb…`:

> "Each model's monthly limit below determines how its usage counts toward those allowances."

"Counts **toward those allowances**" is compatible with a per-model budget and with a shared pool the
per-model figure bounds. The page does not say which. Both earlier passes resolved an ambiguity in
favour of the reading that flatters the number, without saying they had done so.

**Candidate explanations, enumerated before testing.** Three, and each was testable against a named
artefact:

1. *The docs are right and the ceilings are independent.* Refutable by any report of a model
   blocked while its own usage is near zero.
2. *The ceilings are a shared pool.* Refutable by a report of every model blocking at once with
   total spend well under the pool.
3. *The meter sums per-model percentages rather than dividing dollars by the pool.* Refutable by a
   report whose displayed percentages sum to 100 while its dollars sit far below the pool.

**What settled it.** All three fired, which is the reason this is a range and not a verdict:

| # | Date | Verbatim |
|---|---|---|
| `anomalyco/opencode#49186` (open) | 2026-09-15 | "the usage was actually deducted from the same shared Go monthly quota, which caused approximately 20% of my monthly allowance to be consumed unexpectedly" |
| `anomalyco/opencode#47547` (open) | 2026-09-06 | "Total actual spend: $22.18 out of the $60 monthly limit = ~37%." … "calculated by summing the per-model percentages (47.8 + 34.7 + 17.5 = exactly 100.0%)" |
| `anomalyco/opencode#52962` (closed as dup) | 2026-10-03 | "every other paid model — **including models with zero recorded usage** — returns `Go usage limit exceeded`. Total paid spend at that point was **$4.38**." |
| `anomalyco/opencode#46365` (open) | 2026-08-31 | "Monthly Usage: 100% while the sum of all model usages is only about **$24.53**" |
| r/opencode `1wy907r` | 2026-10-05 | "I seem to hit my monthly limit on 20$, when ive only used my sub on glm, deepseek and mimo." |
| r/opencode `1wy6vby` | 2026-10-05 | "7.56$ usage = 27% with these calculations i get about **28$** worth of usage per month" |

**The third explanation is the one that would have been missed.** Had "shared pool" been accepted on
the strength of the first four issues and moved on, #47547 would have been filed under it. Its
percentages sum to *exactly* 100.0 while its dollars are at 37%, and no shared-pool model predicts
that. It is a **different mechanism** — a display/aggregation bug — and it is the reason the honest
answer is a range rather than "the pool is $60 shared".

**Independent corroboration.** `FeiZhuLulu/real-api-pricing` records the model per row in its
`data/adopted.csv`: `min(共享月池$60, 模型Usage $60)` with `同套餐各模型额度不可相加` — "per-model
allowances within one plan are not additive". That repository's own `tools/extract-plans.py` writes a
`min(shared pool, per-model cap)` expression into every subscription row, so the shared-pool semantics
were encoded there before this research began. Independent of the issue reports, and it agrees with
them.

**Decisive arithmetic on the vendor's own page**, which needs no issue tracker: **Go Plus raises the
price 4x ($10 → $40) and the DeepSeek per-model cap only 2x ($60 → $60 against a $120 pool)**. If
the caps were independent budgets, buying Go Plus would double that row's yield. It does not. That
single line is the cleanest evidence available and it is first-party.

**Correction made.** `2026-10-02/data/plan-economics.csv` carries `SHARED-POOL` in every
dollar-ceiling row's basis with the issue numbers in the notes; `2026-09-20`'s row and README carry
dated superseding notes; `2026-10-06/data/plan-economics.csv` adds `cap_model`,
`monthly_pool_usd`, `per_model_cap_usd` and separate `tokens_m_advertised` / `tokens_m_measured`
columns. The planning number is now **6,211M advertised / 2,070–2,899M measured** on the qualifying
lane, converted in [../2026-10-06/data/pool-meter-reports.csv](../2026-10-06/data/pool-meter-reports.csv)
from the two archived r/opencode quotes.

**Limit, stated plainly.** No maintainer has replied in any of these threads, the vendor's page is
ambiguous, and this pass has no paid account. The pool model is the best-supported reading and it is
not settled.

## Review 4 — A unit error that reached a headline verdict

**Defect.** A figure of `25.81亿` was read as **25.81 billion** when the stored row said
`monthly_tokens: 2581000000`. 亿 is 10^8, so 25.81亿 **is** 2.581 billion: a 10x error that flipped a
HIT to a PARTIAL only when a second analyst re-derived the arithmetic against the stored row.

**Why it survived, which is the point.** Two independent analysts made the same mis-read from the
same file. Reading a number is not checking one, and care is not the missing ingredient: re-deriving
from a stored row is.

**What this pass does about it.** `unit-scale` compares column pairs that state the same quantity in
different units, and converts rather than copies. `tools/derive-yields.py` prints the whole division
so the arithmetic is inspectable rather than asserted.

**Correct-input demonstration of the arithmetic itself**, at the wall this pass is built on:

```
$ python3 tools/derive-yields.py --ceiling 10 --input 0.10 --output 0.20 --cache 0.002 --mix 100/0/0
ceiling      = $10  (independent per-model ceiling; use --ceiling-basis min --pool P --cap C where a pool exists)
prices       = input $0.1  output $0.2  cache read $0.002 per 1M
mix          = 100% cache read / 0% fresh input / 0% output

  blended $/M = (1*0.002 + 0*0.1 + 0*0.2)
             = 0.002000 $/M

  yield       = $10 / 0.002000 $/M
             = 5,000 M tokens/month = 5.000 B
```

**A figure this pass refused to publish.** The same body of work asserts that "10B of DeepSeek V4.1
Flash at list costs ~$444/month, so a $10→10B route needs a ~44x multiplier". That implies
$0.0444/M. It is not reproducible from any price source this pass can verify: DeepSeek's own
published off-peak tariff [R5] blends to $0.01559/M under the standard mix ($155.90 for 10B, 15.6x),
the peak tariff to $0.03118/M ($311.80, 31.2x), and OpenCode Go's subsidised DeepSeek rate to
$0.00966/M ($96.60, 9.7x). **The figure is not carried**, and the wall is published at the rate that
*is* reproducible: 5,000M on the cheapest qualifying price, needing a 2x multiplier over raw credit,
against 9.7x–15.6x on the model a buyer would actually purchase. The conclusion is unchanged and it
does not need the number this pass cannot source.

### 5.4 Three bugs in the new checks, found by running them

1. **`unit-scale` was confidently wrong on correct data.** Its first version compared *every* token
   column to every other and reported the correct `tokens_m_advertised=6211` / `tokens_m_measured=2900`
   row as a 2.1x magnitude error. Those two columns are deliberately different quantities: a vendor
   ceiling and a user meter. The check now compares only column pairs that state one quantity, and
   excludes any column whose name ends `_measured`. Same lesson as the 2026-10-02 pass's
   substring-matched column bug, and the same lesson: **only running a check against known-good data
   surfaces a check that is confidently wrong.**
2. **`unit-scale` crashed the gate on a Windows console.** Printing the CJK suffix in a failure
   message raised `UnicodeEncodeError` under cp1252, so the gate died with a traceback instead of
   reporting failures. Failure text is now ASCII.
3. **`shared-cap` and `unscored-model` did not fire on their own planted defects.** `shared-cap` read
   `meter_basis`, which the 2026-10-06 schema does not have (it has `cap_model`), and
   `unscored-model` required a score column that this pass does not carry (the score lives in
   `aa-lookup.csv`). Both were rewritten to read either column and to fire on an *asserted*
   provenance as well as a published number. Neither would have been found without the planted run.

### 5.5 The gate also cannot be run on Windows without a caveat, and that is a finding

The three CI reproducibility checks pass on Linux. On this host they appear to fail, and the cause
is **not** a content change:

```
$ python3 tools/parse-opencode-go.py 2026-10-02/sources/opencode-go.html > /tmp/grid.csv
$ diff -u 2026-10-02/data/opencode-go-grid.csv /tmp/grid.csv
Files ... differ
```

Byte inspection shows the parser writes `lineterminator="\r\n"` and Windows text-mode stdout then
translates each `\n` into a second `\r`, producing `\r\r\n`. Normalising that one artefact makes the
files byte-identical:

```
committed sha: 779312571664b6f70f79  (6504 bytes)
normalised sha: 779312571664b6f70f79  (6583 -> 6504 bytes)
BYTE-IDENTICAL after normalising \r\r\n -> \r\n: True
```

The committed CSV, its archived source page, and all three reproducibility artefacts are **unmodified**
by this pass (`git diff --stat` over them is empty). This is recorded because a future reader on
Windows will hit it and conclude CI is broken. It is not; CI runs on `ubuntu-latest`. The fix, if it
is ever wanted, is for the parsers to write bytes rather than text-mode stdout.

## Review 6 — The relay class, measured instead of excluded

**The prior position.** The 2026-09-20 pass excluded relays and resellers **by category**: "API
relays, sponsor marketplaces and account resellers are excluded from rankings by policy … because
their discounts are ToS-violating quota resale and their pricing is advertisement, not a published
rate card." The 2026-10-02 pass repeated the conclusion: "Do not buy anything from a relay or
reseller."

**Why that is a defect and not a stance.** Excluding a class by category means the class is never
measured, and the reasons given are assertions about what the class *is* rather than observations of
what the members *do*. The red-flag register in `2026-09-20/data/relay-market-flags.csv` is sound and
is kept. What was missing is the opposite test: if a member publishes a rate card, is its number
cheap or not?

**Result under a four-part standard** (published rate card or allowance; quantified reputation;
documented delivery ceiling; failure modes recorded from issue evidence), in
[../2026-10-06/data/relay-providers.csv](../2026-10-06/data/relay-providers.csv):

| Provider | Public rate card | Public plan price | Rankable | Excluded because |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fenno AI | **yes**, 18 models at a public endpoint | no — login wall | no | price not published; the only credit coupon converts against an inflated meter |
| PPToken | no | no — login wall | no | no rate card, no price, no documented ceiling |
| CCTK.AI | no | no — login wall | no | no rate card, no price, no documented ceiling |
| Sub2api (the gateway stack most of this roster runs) | yes, code is public | deployment is free | n/a | software, not a provider — see below |

**Every exclusion is now a measured property, not a category.**

**The one measurable member, and what its number says.** Fenno's public endpoint was fetched today
(HTTP 200, 11,905 B, archived). `deepseek-v4-1-flash` prices at `input_price` 8e-07,
`output_price` 3.2e-06, `cache_read_price` 1.6e-08 per token — $0.80 / $3.20 / $0.016 per 1M. At the
standard mix that is **$0.05152/M**, against DeepSeek's own published off-peak list of **$0.01559/M**
for the same model: **3.30x the official price**. $10 buys **194M tokens**, missing a 10B bar by
**51x**. The relay is 3.3x *more* expensive than buying from the vendor, and that is now a
measurement rather than a red flag.

**Two further measured facts about that rate card**, both read out of the archived JSON:
`availability.status` is `"unknown"` with `request_count: 0` and `data_status: "pending"` on every
model — the platform has never recorded a request on any model it lists. And the advertised
"0.16x rate multiplier … roughly 2.2% of official pricing" in the sponsor roster **fails its own
arithmetic**: 0.16x is 16%, not 2.2%. An offer that cannot multiply is not evidence of cheapness, which
was the 2026-09-20 pass's own argument and it survives the standard.

**Sub2api's mechanics, from its code** (`backend/internal/service/user_subscription.go`, commit
`b8dece9`):

```go
func (s *UserSubscription) CheckMonthlyLimit(group *Group, additionalCost float64) bool {
	if !group.HasMonthlyLimit() { return true }
	return s.MonthlyUsageUSD+additionalCost <= *group.MonthlyLimitUSD
}
```

called from `CheckUsageLimits` in `subscription_service.go`, which returns `ErrMonthlyLimitExceeded`
and runs as a middleware pre-check. Two consequences, both verified against the codebase:

- **No proportional seat-sharing exists.** Grepping `backend/` for `seat` / `CarPool` / `proportional`
  / `share_quota` returns only OAuth fingerprint tests. There is no per-seat divisor anywhere. A
  subscriber's share is an **operator-chosen dollar allowance**, not a computed fraction of a seat.
  This corrects the framing that a pooled seat can be split fairly, and it is the mechanism behind
  the arithmetic in the 2026-10-06 report's seat-splitting result: `(P/N)/(Q/N) = P/Q`, so N-way
  splitting cannot create a multiplier.
- **Enforcement is a hard cap, pre-request**, which makes the runaway risk low for a subscriber and
  high for the operator's upstream accounts.

**Failure modes, from its own tracker, with numbers:** quota shrinkage under pooling (#5692, 24
comments — a Pro 20x account's usable weekly quota jumping from 85% to 98% consumed; #5786 — accounts
normal on official Codex losing quota after being pooled and recovering when the gateway is switched
off); ban risk (#1141, #3624 — the TLS-fingerprint template is gated to Anthropic OAuth/SetupToken
only at `account.go:1695`, so OpenAI OAuth requests go over a plain Go transport — #3896, #6134, #6180,
#6755, #6892); silent substitution and degradation (#7503 attributes it to the gateway's TLS
fingerprint); availability storms (#6739, 49 comments — "Our servers are currently overloaded" from a
pool running under 3 concurrent per account; #593).

**A counter reading, tested.** The sponsor roster advertises "no intelligence degradation", "no
model mixing" and "transparent usage and billing". That is a claim about behaviour, so it was
checked against the issue tracker rather than accepted or dismissed, and it is contradicted by #7503
and the degradation cluster. **The marketing claim is the unmeasured one.**

**Verdict: PASS WITH DISCLOSURE.** The class is now measured, 0 of 4 evaluated providers are
rankable, and the reasons are properties. The disclosure is that the standard is applied to four
operators, which is the entire roster this pass could reach with a public surface; the roster itself
carries 23 sponsors, of which 14 sell model quota and 9 sell proxies or CDN services that are out of
scope for token economics.

## What this pass did not establish

- **No plan was subscribed to and no authenticated request was made.** Every token figure is
  arithmetic on a vendor's own published numbers. Nothing here is a metered result.
- **Whether OpenCode Go's monthly limits are pooled is not settled.** Six reports say shared, the
  vendor's page is ambiguous, no maintainer has replied, and settling it needs a paid account. It is
  carried as 6,211M advertised against 2,070–2,899M measured, not as a single figure.
- **`#47547` suggests a second, distinct mechanism** — a meter that sums per-model percentages rather
  than dividing dollars by the pool — which would make the effective ceiling move rather than sit at a
